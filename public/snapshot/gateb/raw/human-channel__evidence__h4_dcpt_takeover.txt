==================================================================================
HUMAN CHANNEL H4 - distraction and takeover response in L3 automation (DCPT)
takeover time = reaction to resume control (s); TOR issued at random time
==================================================================================

trials 1080, participants 40

### takeover time by non-driving task ###
  ALL tasks                  n=1080  mean 2.23s  median 2.10s  sd 0.80
  1 no task                  n= 117  mean 1.99s  median 1.80s  sd 0.83
  2 watching video           n= 120  mean 2.01s  median 1.90s  sd 0.49
  3 playing game             n= 123  mean 2.49s  median 2.30s  sd 1.01
  4 messaging                n= 124  mean 2.51s  median 2.50s  sd 0.67
  5 phone call               n= 113  mean 2.17s  median 2.10s  sd 0.69
  6 radio                    n= 120  mean 2.03s  median 1.90s  sd 0.69
  7 reading                  n= 121  mean 2.48s  median 2.50s  sd 0.73
  8 eating                   n= 116  mean 2.36s  median 2.10s  sd 1.05
  9 chatting                 n= 126  mean 2.05s  median 2.00s  sd 0.58

### grouped by how the task loads the driver ###
  no task (baseline)         n= 117  mean 1.99s  median 1.80s  sd 0.83
  cognitive-only (5,6,9)     n= 359  mean 2.08s  median 2.00s  sd 0.65
  visual-manual (3,4,7,8)    n= 484  mean 2.46s  median 2.30s  sd 0.88

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
visual-manual vs no-task: 2.46s vs 1.99s, +0.47s (+24%). Eyes/hands off the road slows the takeover.
cognitive-only vs no-task: 2.08s vs 1.99s, +0.09s. Attention load with eyes freer costs less.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

response, not the cross-agent dependence; descriptive across participants, not clustered;
takeover time is reaction to resume control, not a collision outcome.
