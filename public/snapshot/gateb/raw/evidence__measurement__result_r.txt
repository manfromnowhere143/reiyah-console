==============================================================================================
RESULT R - does the coupling rise with detector accuracy, or is that Result P again?
camera = Mapillary (fixed score>=0.3); lidars = PointPillars(29.5), Megvii(51.9),
CenterPoint(57.4, produced+validated here); L5 five confounders; instance-clustered
bootstrap 1,500 reps seed 20260828
==============================================================================================

PROVENANCE
  tool     : tools/measure/result_r_accuracy_coupling.py
  question : At each lidar's own default score>=0.3 the conditional coefficient rises with
             lidar accuracy (1.096 -> 1.151 -> 1.216). Result P showed c is deflated by the
             marginal miss rates, and a stronger detector has a LOWER miss rate, which inflates
             c mechanically. So the raw trend is confounded and cannot be claimed as coupling.
  method   : compare the lidars at a MATCHED marginal miss rate. Threshold each lidar to a
             common global miss rate P_B, hold the camera fixed at 0.3, recompute the L5
             conditional coefficient. If c still rises with accuracy at equal miss rates, the
             effect is real; if it flattens, the raw trend was the Result P artifact.
  CenterPoint validation: devkit mAP 0.5740; match.py reaggregation 57.47 vs 57.40 (PASS).

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING - stated with the confound named and quantified
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOST OF THE RAW TREND IS THE RESULT P MARGINAL ARTIFACT. The raw spread across the accuracy
   range is 0.120; at a matched miss rate it collapses to about 0.02. Roughly four fifths of the
   apparent "stronger detectors couple more" is the arithmetic of c under a lower marginal, not
   coincident failure. This is the confound Result P predicted, now measured.

2. A SMALL, ROBUST RESIDUAL SURVIVES. At every one of the four matched miss-rate levels the
   ordering is perfectly monotonic in accuracy (CenterPoint > Megvii > PointPillars), and at the
   extremes the 95% intervals separate (e.g. at P_B=0.40, PointPillars 1.116 [1.106,1.124] vs
   CenterPoint 1.141 [1.129,1.149], non-overlapping). So there is a genuine accuracy-coupling
   effect - the strongest detector fails together with the camera slightly more than the weakest,
   even at equal miss rates - but it is an order of magnitude smaller than the naive comparison.

3. CONSEQUENCE. The intuition that "better sensors couple more" is directionally supported but
   quantitatively tiny once the marginal artifact is removed. Any safety argument that reads a
   raw accuracy-coupling trend must first match marginals; most of that trend is not coupling.
