FINDING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Across the operating range 0.1 to 0.5, for both detector pairs, the RSS coincidence constant c
and the absolute joint-miss rate P(both) move in OPPOSITE directions, with non-overlapping
bootstrap intervals at every step:

  megvii pair       c 2.271 -> 1.239 (down 1.03)   P(both) 0.096 -> 0.509 (up, ~5.3x)
  pointpillars pair c 1.909 -> 1.193 (down 0.72)   P(both) 0.104 -> 0.505 (up, ~4.8x)

The absolute excess joint-miss over independence, P(both) - P_A P_B, rises from the loose end
and plateaus in the 0.3-0.4 band (megvii 0.054 -> 0.099; pointpillars 0.050 -> 0.088); it never
follows c downward. So at every operating point at or above 0.2 the absolute excess is far above
its 0.1 value while c is below its 0.1 value.

Reading, stated as a property of the estimand and not as a safety determination:

  1. c is a ratio deflated by the rising marginal miss rates. As P_A and P_B rise toward 1 the
     denominator P_A P_B rises, dragging c toward 1 for arithmetic reasons, exactly the
     non-invariance the estimand doc names. Near the marginal-rate boundary any two events look
     independent by this ratio.

  2. The safety-relevant quantity is not the ratio. A real object missed by both sensors is
     missed whatever c says. P(both) and its absolute excess are what a redundancy argument must
     bound, and they are largest where c is smallest.

  3. Therefore a redundancy argument that certifies safety by showing c near 1 - which is the
     shape of RSS Corollary 3's premise - can hold at exactly the operating point where the two
     sensors jointly miss the most objects. c must be read together with the marginal miss
     rates, never alone.
